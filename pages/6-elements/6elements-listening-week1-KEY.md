### Q5 and Q9 are a matched pair — teach them together

This is the single most confused pair in the unit, so the sheet asks it twice from
opposite sides.

- **Q5 is DURATION.** The envelope *shape* is identical. Only the **hold** changed.
  On the ENVELOPE screen the two lines have the same ramps and the same slopes —
  one is just stretched out flat in the middle.
- **Q9 is ENVELOPE.** The **length is identical**. Only the ramp at the front changed.
  On the ENVELOPE screen the two lines end at the same place — one just takes much
  longer to climb.

> **The line that fixes it:** *Duration is how long you hold the button. Envelope is
> what the sound does while you're holding it.*
>
> If a kid gets 5 and 9 both right, they have the hardest distinction in the unit.
> Say that out loud to them.

### Q7 — the one you build the lesson around

**ANSWER: TIMBRE.** Both clips are 220 Hz. The fundamental never moves.

B has the 2nd and 4th partials removed, which makes it hollow — and *hollow* reads as
*different note* to an untrained ear. Expect a majority to say PITCH on the first pass.

**Do not correct them with words.** Point at the leftmost bar on the SPECTRUM screen and
play A, then B. It sits in exactly the same place. The bars *behind* it are what moved,
and the green shading is sitting on those bars, not on the first one.

### Q11 — reverb is not volume

**ANSWER: SPACE.** The level is locked at 0.70 in both clips. Verify it out loud if they
argue — it's in the bank, `a:{reverb:0}` and `b:{reverb:.85}`, nothing else.

B *feels* quieter because farther-away things are quieter in real life, so their ears
run that inference automatically. That inference is correct about the world and wrong
about the knob, which is a genuinely interesting thing to say to a 12-year-old.

# PART TWO — WHICH DIRECTION

**2 — PITCH**
- Pitch: **lower** (A is 330 Hz, B is 165 Hz — a full octave down)
- Wavelength: **longer**

> The two blanks disagree on purpose. Lower pitch = fewer waves per second = each wave
> has more room = **longer**. This is the backwards one. About half the room gets the
> first blank right and the second one wrong, which tells you they're pattern-matching
> "lower → shorter" instead of thinking. On the SPECTRUM, B's bar sits **left** of A's.

**4 — VOLUME**
- Amplitude: **bigger** (A is 0.28, B is 0.80)
- Bars got: **taller**

> Taller, not wider. Volume is height on a spectrum. Nothing moved sideways — that's
> the whole point, and it's the visual proof that volume and pitch are independent.

**6 — DURATION**
- Clip B is: **sudden**

> Note that 5 and 6 are **reversed** — in Q5 the long one was B, here it's A. That's
> deliberate. Anyone who circled "sustained" out of rhythm wasn't listening, and you now
> know who.

**8 — TIMBRE**
- Clip B has: **more overtones**
- Pure tone: **A**

> A is `parts:[1]` — a single sine wave. One bar on the spectrum and nothing else, which
> is the cleanest picture of "fundamental" they will ever see. Pull this one up on the
> projector even if you skip everything else. A pure tone is rare in nature and it
> sounds *wrong* to them — worth naming why: real sounds have overtones.

**10 — ENVELOPE**
- Clip B sounds like someone: **hit a drum**
- Slower attack: **A**

> A has a 0.7-second attack. B has 0.002. Same note, same loudness — and one is
> percussion and one is a string section. This is the "envelope decides what instrument
> it is" moment. If you demo one thing from Part Two, demo this.

**12 — SPACE**
- Clip B sounds: **closer**

> Reversed from Q11, same reason as Q6. Dry = close. Wet = far.

# PART THREE — EXPLAIN IT

**13 — CREDIT IF** the word **reflections** appears and the explanation connects
reflections to *distance* or *room size.*

**Full credit looks like:** *"The reverb adds reflections bouncing off the room, so your
brain thinks it's far away even though the volume is the same."*

**Half credit:** *"There's more reverb so it sounds farther."* True, but it restates the
question. Push once: **what is reverb made of?**

**No credit:** *"It got quieter."* That's the misconception, not an explanation.

> **Worth saying to the class:** the direct sound is identical in both clips. Everything
> added in B arrived *late*, because it went the long way around the room first. That
> delay is the only information your brain needs to place a sound in space.

# PART FOUR — YOUR CALL

**14 — CREDIT IF** they pick a clip, name an element, and the reason connects the two.
**The pick itself is not graded.**

Most will pick **A** — slow attack, long release, wet with reverb. The expected element
is **ENVELOPE** (the slow build is the dread) or **SPACE** (the reverb is the empty
hallway). Both are correct. Both are in the clip.

> **Accept B and mean it.** A kid who picks the short dry hit and says *"jump scares
